**Bonesaw** is a blog of communist history and critique. The name is
literal. The bone in question is the calcified shell that working-class
parties tend to grow around themselves once they confuse organisational
self-preservation with the movement they were built to serve. Rosa
Luxemburg saw this tendency early. Trotsky in 1904 named its terminal
form. Hal Draper in 1971 called the result the "micro-sect."[^1] We
borrow from all three and from those they argued with.

The editorial line, such as it is, holds that:

- Centralism is a tool, not a creed. Its value lies in coordination,
  not in obedience.
- Bureaucracy is not an aberration grafted onto otherwise healthy
  parties. It is what parties become when their environment turns
  hostile and their cadre stop being held accountable from below.
- The historical record of twentieth-century communism is the property
  of the working class, not the property of those who would either
  whitewash it or use it to declare the whole project finished.

Posts here cite their sources. Where a claim cannot be verified, the
post says so.
